Mainnet escrow is unavailable pending deployment/verification. The guard returns `503 escrow_unavailable` or `escrow_misconfigured` before authentication/chain execution. **No mainnet dispute or Safe decision was submitted.**

## Configured request

`POST /api/escrow/dispute` accepts `reference`, `callerSCA` and optional `reason`, defaulting to `No reason provided`.

The stored row must be ACTIVE with a contract ID. `callerSCA` must match depositor or beneficiary, and `verifyCallerControlsAddress` must authorize that party under the applicable merchant/consumer/internal-agent identity. Consumer step-up remains required where applicable. Being the escrow's creating merchant is not permission to impersonate its beneficiary.

Circle execution submits `dispute(bytes32,string)`. External merchant wallets instead receive a queued transaction with `pendingSignature: true`; their wallet must broadcast and supply verified evidence. Non-merchant branches still use Circle, not a universal external-wallet resume flow.

After Circle completion, success returns the updated DISPUTED row, transaction hash, explorer URL and message. The route attempts an unsigned best-effort webhook. A queued request or delivered notification alone does not prove the contract is disputed. Reconcile the receipt/event and contract state independently.

## Resolution and custody

A dispute **does not move funds**: the deposit remains in the escrow contract. Normal confirmation and expiry refund cannot bypass a disputed record. The locally tested contract lets its administrator resolve to the beneficiary or depositor; it does not provide an arbitrary split/third-party payout.

Administrator identity and Safe acceptance must be verified after owner deployment. The existing server EOA signing path cannot sign on behalf of a Safe. An API message saying “admin will review” is not a resolution guarantee, automatic refund or Safe transaction submission.

No client idempotency key or atomic chain/database dispute lifecycle exists here. A failed response may follow a successful transaction. Inspect the original operation before retrying or taking another funds action.
